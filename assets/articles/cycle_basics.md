# Understanding Your Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a monthly sequence of hormonal events coordinated between your brain and ovaries. While 28 days is often cited as the textbook length, a healthy cycle typically ranges anywhere between 21 and 35 days.

## The Four Key Phases

### 1. Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)
The cycle begins on the first day of full menstrual bleeding. Progesterone and estrogen levels drop, signaling the uterine lining (endometrium) to shed.
- **Common sensations:** Mild cramps, lower energy, desire to rest.
- **Tip:** Prioritize hydration and iron-rich meals (spinach, lentils, seeds).

### 2. Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)
Overlapping with your period, the pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to mature. One dominant follicle will release an egg.
- **Common sensations:** Rising estrogen boosts energy, mood, and skin glow.
- **Tip:** Great time for creative planning and progressive workouts.

### 3. Ovulation Phase (Around Day 14)
A sudden surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the dominant follicle to release its egg into the fallopian tube. The egg survives for 12 to 24 hours.
- **Fertile Window:** Sperm can survive up to 5 days in fertile cervical mucus, making the 5 days before ovulation and the day of ovulation your peak fertile window.

### 4. Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)
The ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, producing progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for potential implantation.
- **Common sensations:** If pregnancy does not occur, progesterone drops, potentially causing PMS symptoms (bloating, mood sensitivity, breast tenderness).
